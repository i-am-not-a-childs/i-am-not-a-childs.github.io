What I Learned From World Models

The paper this article is on is called World Models. This paper essentially shows that AI can be taught how the world works, and it can use that knowledge to create a simulator to train itself to complete its task, whatever that may be. I feel that world models could be used in my game to allow the AI controlling the robot to simulate ways of attack more and more as the game progresses, teaching it new things, and allowing it to beat almost any enemy. The robots in my game would most likely use world models to practice against different kinds of enemies, while also getting itself used to its own abilities. I learned a lot from this article, including what I have already said. Some more things that I have learned is that, sometimes, the AI can create the world model incorrectly and with a glitch. This would cause the AI to learn the wrong way to beat the game, and in the actual game, it would fail miserably and in my game, be utterly destroyed. To correct this, the authors suggested that there should be some randomness to the model. For example, in a racing game, it could have different wind velocities, or different levels of traction on the track. This "noise" makes it so that the AI can't memorize one perfect strategy, it has to learn something that works in lots of different situations. That transfers much better to the real world. All of this could dramatically help a robot, because it could train in its own world, which is much cheaper and it saves the robot's durability and energy. One cool thing about this system is that the AI doesn't have to remember everything about the arena, it can just rebember the important things. For example, in my game, all the AI would need to remember is the weapons and the target. It could ignore everything else.



What I Learned From PlaNet: Learning Latent Dynamics for Planning from Pixels

The paper this article is about is called PlaNet: Learning Latent Dynamics for Planning from Pixels. To summarize, this article shows that using world models, AI can plan and predict what will happen next, whcih can greatly reduce the amount of real-world experiences ending in failure. The AI in Robattles could use PlaNet to plan ahead, which could make the player's robot more efficient. In the game, I feel that PlaNet could be an upgrade. Some interesting things I have learned beside what I have already said are that, PlaNet learns both the predictable and the unpredictable parts of the world. It also makes long term predictions to make much better decisions. PlaNet can help robots by allowing them to predict what might happen next in the world, which in turn allows them to make better decisions, letting them waste less time, money, and energy. My final takeaway is that PlaNet makes multiple predictions with a few key numbers, representing pixels from the images of the world. It trains itself to predict many steps into the future, making long-term planning more accurate. These predictions can help robots in Robattles to make life-or-death decisions such as if they should avoid an attack, or if they can survive it and take down the enemy.
