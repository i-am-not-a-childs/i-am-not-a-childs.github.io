What I Learned From World Models

The paper this article is on is called World Models. This paper essentially shows that AI can be taught how the world works, and it can use that knowledge to create a simulator to train itself to complete its task, whatever that may be. I feel that world models could be used in my game to allow the AI controlling the robot to simulate ways of attack more and more as the game progresses, teaching it new things, and allowing it to beat almost any enemy. The robots in my game would most likely use world models to practice against different kinds of enemies, while also getting itself used to its own abilities. Sometimes, the AI can create the world model incorrectly and with a glitch. This would cause the AI to learn the wrong way to beat the game, and in the actual game, it would fail miserably and in my game, be utterly destroyed. To correct this, the authors suggested that there should be some randomness to the model. For example, in a racing game, it could have different wind velocities, or different levels of traction on the track. This "noise" makes it so that the AI can't memorize one perfect strategy, it has to learn something that works in lots of different situations. That transfers much better to the real world. All of this could dramatically help a robot, because it could train in its own world, which is much cheaper and it saves the robot's durability and energy. One cool thing about this system is that the AI doesn't have to remember everything about the arena, it can just rebember the important things. For example, in my game, all the AI would need to remember is the weapons and the target. It could ignore everything else.

What I Learned From PlaNet: Learning Latent Dynamics for Planning from Pixels

The paper this article is about is called PlaNet: Learning Latent Dynamics for Planning from Pixels. To summarize, this article shows that using world models, AI can plan and predict what will happen next, whcih can greatly reduce the amount of real-world experiences ending in failure. The AI in Robattles could use PlaNet to plan ahead, which could make the player's robot more efficient. In the game, I feel that PlaNet could be an upgrade. PlaNet learns both the predictable and the unpredictable parts of the world. It also makes long term predictions to make much better decisions. PlaNet can help robots by allowing them to predict what might happen next in the world, which in turn allows them to make better decisions, letting them waste less time, money, and energy. My final takeaway is that PlaNet makes multiple predictions with a few key numbers, representing pixels from the images of the world. It trains itself to predict many steps into the future, making long-term planning more accurate. These predictions can help robots in Robattles to make life-or-death decisions such as if they should avoid an attack, or if they can survive it and take down the enemy. What I Learned From Dream to Control: Learning Behaviors by Latent Imagination

The paper this article is on is called Dream to Control: Learning Behaviors by Latent Imagination. Instead of predicting multiple futures like PlaNet, Dreamer imagines what would happen for each choice that it has. Dreamer imagines millions of outcomes and it uses their successes and failures to train a driving policy. That policy allows it to make choices without worrying about everything. For example, when you first learn how to ride a bike, you worry about pedaling, turning, and staying balanced, but once you are experienced, you don't worry about those things, you have a policy that you use to ride without thinking, without planning every step of the way. This would allow robots in Robattles to imagine what would happen after each decision, it would know to avoid attacks and when to attack itself. Dreamer uses "Latex Imagination," which is basically saying that instead of using images, Dreamer uses the ideas from the images. Suppose the robot imagines: Go Left --> Crash. The computer asks: "Exactly what part of my decision caused the crash?" It then adjusts itself a tiny bit. Do that millions of times, and it gradually becomes better. It's like a student taking practice tests. Every wrong answer teaches them how to improve. The computer just does this with math instead of pencil and paper. This is much more efficient compared to older reinforcement learning. This would allow robots to get better results with much less real-world experience compared to World Models and PlaNet. How Can I Use World Models in Robattles

I can use world models in Robattles to control the robots. The world model will tell the robots when to attack, when not to attack, when to dodge, when to charge at their opponent head on, etc. A world model is basically a simulation that an AI creates based on its real-world experiences. AI uses world models to get better at its task. It would be hard for my game without world models, because the AI would have to know both it's health, and the enemy's health. Plus, the AI would have to learn how to use different weapons and it would have to get itself accustomed to each loadout that the player makes. World models can help by allowing the AI to practice, which can dramatically up the AI's performance. For my first prototype, I would have the AI only practice moving, and all it would need to focus on are the walls, it would only need to predict enough to not crash. I wouldn't try to teach it evading or attcking just yet, because I feel that it would be too complicated for me right now. Next, I want to learn about how to use and make world models. This will allow me to understant what is happening when I am actually incorporating the AI into my game. If I had three months to build something, I would build the world model and train it to move around the arena and to avoid arena traps.
