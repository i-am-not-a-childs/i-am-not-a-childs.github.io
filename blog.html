What I Learned From World Models

The paper this article is on is called World Models. This paper essentially shows that AI can be taught how the world works, and it can use that knowledge to create a simulator to train itself to complete its task, whatever that may be. I feel that World Models could be used in my game to allow the AI controlling the robot to simulate ways of attack more and more as the game progresses, teaching it new things, and allowing it to beat almost any enemy. The robots in my game would most likely use World Models to practice against different kinds of enemies, while also getting itself used to its own abilities. I learned a lot from this article, including what I have already said. Some more things that I have learned is that, sometimes, the AI can create the World Model incorrectly and with a glitch. This would cause the AI to learn the wrong way to beat the game, and in the actual game, it would fail miserably and in my game, be utterly destroyed. To correct this, the authors suggested that there should be some randomness to the Model. For example, in a racing game, it could have different wind velocities, or different levels of traction to the track. This noise makes it so that the AI can't memorize one perfect strategy, it has to learn something that works in lots of different situations. That transfers much better to the real world. All of this could dramatically help a robot, because it could train in its own world, which is much cheaper and it saves the robot's durability and energy. One cool thing about this system is that, the AI doesn't have to remember everything about the arena, it can just rebember the important things. For example, in my game, all the AI would need to remember is the weapons and the target. It could ignore everything else.
